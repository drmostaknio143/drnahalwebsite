**EN — Title:** `Cataract Surgery Recovery: What to Do (and Avoid) in the First Weeks`

**Meta description:** `A clear, practical guide to cataract surgery recovery — what's normal, what to avoid, and when to call your doctor.`

**Body:**

`Cataract surgery is one of the most common and safest procedures in modern medicine — but the days after surgery matter just as much as the procedure itself. How you care for your eye in the first few weeks directly affects how quickly, and how well, you heal.`

`### The First 24 Hours`
`Right after surgery, your eye will be covered with a protective shield — keep it on until your doctor tells you to remove it, including while sleeping for the first night or two. Mild grittiness, watering, and light sensitivity are normal. You should not experience sharp pain — if you do, contact the practice.`

`### What's Normal in the First Week`
`Blurry or fluctuating vision for the first few days is common as your eye adjusts to the new lens. Mild redness and a scratchy feeling usually settle within days. You'll be prescribed antibiotic and anti-inflammatory eye drops — using them exactly as directed, at the right times, matters more than almost anything else for a smooth recovery.`

`### What to Avoid`
`Avoid rubbing or pressing on the eye, even lightly. Skip swimming, hot tubs, and dusty environments for at least two weeks. Avoid heavy lifting or strenuous exercise for about a week, as sudden strain can increase eye pressure. Wear the protective shield at night as instructed, and sunglasses during the day if your eye feels sensitive to light.`

`### When You Can Resume Normal Activities`
`Most patients can watch TV, read, and use their phone right away — this does not strain the eye. Light walking is fine within a day or two. Driving is usually possible once your vision is clear enough and your doctor confirms it, typically within a few days. Return to work often happens within 3–7 days depending on the nature of the job.`

`### Warning Signs — Call Immediately If You Notice:`
`- Sudden, severe pain`
`- A sudden decrease in vision`
`- Increasing redness or discharge`
`- Flashes of light or new floaters`

`These aren't part of a normal recovery and need prompt evaluation.`

`### The Bottom Line`
`Most people see noticeably clearer within a day or two, with full stabilization over a few weeks. Following your drop schedule and protecting the eye from strain, dust, and water gives you the best chance of a smooth, fast recovery.`

**EN — Title:** `7 Questions to Ask Before Getting LASIK`

**Meta description:** `Thinking about LASIK? Here are the questions worth asking your surgeon before you commit.`

**Body:**

`LASIK is a life-changing procedure for many people — but it's also permanent, which means the decision deserves real information, not just enthusiasm. Here are seven questions worth asking before you book.`

`### 1. Am I actually a candidate?`
`Not everyone is. Candidacy depends on a stable prescription for at least a year, sufficient corneal thickness, and no active eye disease like severe dry eye or keratoconus. This can only be confirmed through a proper pre-surgery evaluation — not a quick look.`

`### 2. Which technique is right for me — LASIK, PRK, or SMILE?`
`Each has trade-offs. LASIK offers the fastest visual recovery but requires a corneal flap. PRK is often better for thinner corneas but takes longer to heal. SMILE is minimally invasive with no flap. The right choice depends on your specific eye measurements, not personal preference alone.`

`### 3. What are the realistic risks?`
`Dry eye, glare or halos at night (usually temporary), and under- or over-correction are the most common. Serious complications are rare with an experienced surgeon, but "rare" isn't "zero" — a good surgeon will discuss this honestly rather than only the success stories.`

`### 4. What happens if my vision isn't perfect afterward?`
`Ask about enhancement/touch-up policies. Most practices offer a follow-up correction if needed within a certain window — know this before you commit.`

`### 5. What does recovery actually look like for my job/lifestyle?`
`If you swim competitively, play contact sports, or work in a dusty environment, your recovery timeline and precautions differ from a typical desk job. Be specific about your lifestyle when asking.`

`### 6. What's included in the cost?`
`Ask whether the quoted price includes pre-surgery evaluation, the procedure itself, post-op medications, and follow-up visits — or if these are billed separately.`

`### 7. Can I see the surgeon's actual experience?`
`It's reasonable to ask how many procedures the surgeon has performed and to see examples of their surgical work — many practices, including this one, maintain a video library of real procedures for exactly this reason.`

`### The Bottom Line`
`LASIK's high satisfaction rates come from careful patient selection — not from being right for everyone. A thorough pre-surgery evaluation, not sales pressure, is the sign of a practice worth trusting.`
